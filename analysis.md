# Marketing Unit Economics — Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes the performance of two advertising channels: Search and Social Ads.

The main objective is to evaluate marketing efficiency, compare acquisition costs, measure profitability, and identify opportunities for budget optimization.

The analysis is based on a simulated dataset and a dynamic Excel model.

## 2. Advertising Channel Performance

| Metric | Search | Social Ads |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visitors | 1,000 | 4,000 |
| Orders | 100 | 60 |
| Conversion Rate | 10% | 1.5% |
| Ad Spend | $3,000 | $2,000 |
| CAC | $30.00 | $33.33 |
| Revenue | $5,000 | $3,000 |
| Gross Profit | $3,700 | $2,220 |
| Profit After Marketing | $700 | $220 |
| ROMI | 23.33% | 11% |

Search demonstrates better overall marketing efficiency.

Despite higher advertising spend, Search generates more revenue and profit after marketing. It also achieves a higher conversion rate and lower customer acquisition cost.

## 3. Budget Allocation and Risk Management

Allocating the entire advertising budget to a single channel would increase business risk.

Although Search initially demonstrates better performance, Social Ads also generates positive profit after marketing.

Concentrating all investments in one channel could make the business vulnerable to changes in advertising costs, audience behavior, and campaign performance.

A diversified budget allocation is preferable. Both channels should be monitored, and spending should be adjusted based on performance.

## 4. Experimental Budget Allocation

An additional $1,000 advertising budget was considered for an experimental allocation:

- Search: $400
- Social Ads: $600

The purpose was to evaluate how additional investment would affect each channel's performance.

### Experimental Results

| Metric | Search | Social Ads |
|---|---:|---:|
| Additional Ad Spend | $400 | $600 |
| Additional Profit After Marketing | $60 | $180 |
| Incremental Return on Additional Spend | 15% | 30% |

Social Ads generated a higher incremental return during this experiment.

However, these results should not be treated as proof that Social Ads will always perform better when scaled.

Further testing is required to confirm whether this improvement is sustainable.

## 5. Recommendations

1. Continue investing in both advertising channels.
2. Monitor conversion rate, CAC, ROMI, and profit after marketing.
3. Evaluate marginal returns before increasing advertising budgets.
4. Avoid concentrating the entire budget in one channel.
5. Conduct further experiments before making major budget allocation decisions.

## 6. Limitations

This project uses simulated data for educational purposes.

The experimental results are illustrative and do not represent a real controlled experiment.

The model does not account for all business expenses, such as salaries, rent, taxes, and other operating costs.

Profit After Marketing should therefore not be interpreted as net profit.

Further analysis would require larger datasets, longer observation periods, and controlled experiments.

## 7. Conclusion

Search demonstrates stronger overall performance in the initial dataset, while Social Ads shows higher incremental returns in the illustrative budget experiment.

The results suggest that advertising budget allocation should consider both overall channel profitability and the efficiency of additional investments.

A balanced, data-driven approach can help reduce dependency on a single channel while identifying opportunities for growth.
